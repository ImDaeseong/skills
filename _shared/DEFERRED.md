**A standalone B2B-vs-B2C service skill was evaluated (2026-07-29, same domain-matrix follow-up as the medical/apparel entries above) and deferred, but for a different reason than the others — confirmed with a canonical academic source, then re-verified against each existing skill's actual text (not just its topic).**

*Academic anchor.* Unlike the general business-blog consensus first cited (FourWeekMBA, US Chamber CO-, Finance Strategists, commercetools — real but secondary sources), B2B's defining mechanical difference from B2C has a specific, canonical academic origin: Webster & Wind, "A General Model for Understanding Organizational Buying Behavior," *Journal of Marketing* 36:2 (April 1972), pp. 12-19 — 915 citations per Semantic Scholar (checked 2026-07-29), still the standard reference cited in current B2B-marketing textbooks and industry explainers alike. Its core contribution, the **buying center**, models organizational (B2B) purchases as a multi-role group decision — Initiator, User, Influencer, Decider, Approver, Buyer, Gatekeeper — vs. B2C's single-consumer decision this workspace's existing skills already implicitly assume. This is the precise mechanism behind every derivative claim in the general sources (longer sales cycles, multi-stakeholder decisions, data-driven vs. emotional purchase logic) — citing it directly, not just the summaries built on it, satisfies this workspace's own evidence bar for "논문 기준" (paper-grounded) claims.

*Per-skill re-verification (line-level, re-read directly from each `SKILL.md`, not recalled from memory).* Three of four "already handled" claims from the first pass check out at the text level; one is thinner than first stated and is the actual, actionable gap:
- `distribution/SKILL.md:73` — confirmed: `prospecting` is explicitly tagged "for B2B" as one of six named channel routes in Step 3, alongside implicitly-consumer channels (`directory-submissions`, `public-relations`). Real, holds up.
- `ai_prompt/antigravity_test/skills/cpo_advisor.md:86-89,128` — confirmed: a North Star Metric table explicitly rows "B2B SaaS" vs. "Consumer" vs. "Marketplace," and a separate retention-threshold line sets B2B at 40% D30 vs. consumer at 20%. Real, holds up.
- `biz-ops/SKILL.md` — confirmed: its Attribution routes "commercial deal work (pricing/RFP response/partnerships)" into `alirezarezvani/claude-skills`' Commercial category — B2B by the nature of RFPs and deal-desks, but the word "B2B" itself never appears in the file; the coverage is real but implicit, inherited from what RFP/deal-desk work structurally is, not a stated design choice.
- `biz-council/SKILL.md` — **this is thinner than the first pass claimed.** Step 3a ("Lock the why," line 111) asks "Who is affected? — a demographic/behavior segment... not 'everyone.'" This framing is written for a single buyer/user (B2C-shaped), and never asks the follow-up a B2B idea needs: *who is the user vs. who is the economic buyer vs. who can block the deal* (Webster & Wind's Initiator/User/Decider/Approver/Gatekeeper split). Nothing in Step 1-3 currently prompts the council to separate these roles when the idea being validated is B2B-shaped — an idea's "who is affected" answer could stop at "IT managers at mid-size companies" and never surface that the actual budget-holder or blocking gatekeeper is someone else entirely, a well-documented B2B failure mode this skill has no explicit check against today.
