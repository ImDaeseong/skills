**A standalone B2B-vs-B2C service skill was evaluated (2026-07-29, same domain-matrix follow-up as the medical/apparel entries above) and deferred, but for a different reason than the others — confirmed with a canonical academic source, then re-verified against each existing skill's actual text (not just its topic).**

*Academic anchor.* Unlike the general business-blog consensus first cited (FourWeekMBA, US Chamber CO-, Finance Strategists, commercetools — real but secondary sources), B2B's defining mechanical difference from B2C has a specific, canonical academic origin: Webster & Wind, "A General Model for Understanding Organizational Buying Behavior," *Journal of Marketing* 36:2 (April 1972), pp. 12-19 — 915 citations per Semantic Scholar (checked 2026-07-29), still the standard reference cited in current B2B-marketing textbooks and industry explainers alike. Its core contribution, the **buying center**, models organizational (B2B) purchases as a multi-role group decision — Initiator, User, Influencer, Decider, Approver, Buyer, Gatekeeper — vs. B2C's single-consumer decision this workspace's existing skills already implicitly assume. This is the precise mechanism behind every derivative claim in the general sources (longer sales cycles, multi-stakeholder decisions, data-driven vs. emotional purchase logic) — citing it directly, not just the summaries built on it, satisfies this workspace's own evidence bar for "논문 기준" (paper-grounded) claims.
